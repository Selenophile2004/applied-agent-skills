# Module 1 — Executive / Management Summary

## 1. Role and Boundary

The root `SKILL.md` supplies shared evidence, artifact-state, selective-generation, approval, canonical-source, and handoff rules. This file adds only Module 1 behavior.

Module 1 creates a concise, decision-oriented account of:

1. the organization and relevant context;
2. its current position, including strengths and constraints;
3. consequences, risks, or management implications;
4. the approved solution direction and high-level phases, when available.

Module 1 does not independently design the solution, invent phases, define detailed services, price the engagement, or produce slides.

## 2. Supported Request Modes

Select the narrowest mode that satisfies the request.

| Mode | Use when | Output |
|---|---|---|
| `Full executive summary` | The user needs the complete four-part summary | Context, current position, consequences, and approved solution summary |
| `M1A diagnostic basis` | No solution has been approved yet | Context, current position, consequences, decision question, and approval gate |
| `M1B solution insertion` | M1A and M2 are already approved | A concise M2 summary inserted without rewriting approved M1A |
| `Decision brief` | The immediate need is a Go/No-Go, investment, market-entry, feasibility, or option decision | Situation, complexity/risk, decision questions, and gated approach |
| `Partial section` | The user requests only introduction, current situation, consequences, or another named subsection | Only the requested subsection(s) |
| `Revision` | An existing proposal section must be improved | Revised requested text plus material contradictions or missing evidence, without changing protected sections |

Do not create missing modes merely for completeness. If the user asks for two subsections, produce exactly those two.

## 3. Minimum Inputs

Use available conversation and file context before asking questions. For Module 1, determine:

- organization, organizational level, and relevant business scope;
- proposal audience and decision;
- project trigger and why the issue matters now;
- relevant history, activities, footprint, ownership, or operating context;
- confirmed or reported strengths, assets, capabilities, and positive conditions;
- confirmed or reported constraints, gaps, symptoms, and concerns;
- observed consequences and plausible future risks;
- desired state or management objective;
- approved solution/phases, if any;
- sections of an existing proposal that must remain unchanged.

Ask no more than five consolidated questions, and only when an answer could materially change the summary or recommendation path. If a reliable draft remains possible, proceed with explicit evidence labels or assumptions.

## 4. Preparation Workflow

### 4.1 Parse the request and protect scope

Identify:

- requested mode and subsections;
- intended audience and language;
- expected depth;
- whether the output is a new draft or an edit;
- whether M1A or M2 has already been approved;
- protected text or sections.

For an edit, first state the edit boundary internally: `Change X; preserve Y and Z.`

### 4.2 Build a compact fact-and-claim map

Before drafting, sort only decision-relevant inputs into:

| Bucket | Question |
|---|---|
| Context | What must the reader know about the organization to understand the decision? |
| Enablers | What assets, strengths, momentum, or capabilities can support change? |
| Constraints | What gaps, frictions, risks, or limitations obstruct the desired state? |
| Consequences | What is already happening, and what may happen if the condition persists? |
| Decision | What must management decide or approve? |
| Direction | What approved response exists, if any? |

Apply the shared evidence labels from `SKILL.md`. Do not include a point merely because it appeared in the source; include it only when it affects the decision, diagnosis, or proposed direction.

If the input mixes symptoms, causes, consequences, actions, and decisions, build a compact issue tree internally before drafting. Root it in the governing management question, keep branches at comparable logical levels, and use MECE only where it improves coverage. Do not expose the framework labels unless the user asks for the analytical structure.

### 4.3 Choose the narrative form

Use `Full executive summary` for transformation, strategy, organization design, operating-model, or similar proposals where the reader needs a complete management narrative.

Use `Decision brief` when the immediate question is whether to enter, invest, continue, prioritize, select, or test. The default logic is:

```text
Current situation/opportunity -> management complexity or risk
  -> explicit decision question(s) -> gated approach
```

For multi-entity organizations, determine whether the situation exists at group level, entity level, or both. Do not generalize one subsidiary's condition to the entire group without evidence.

Use SCQA or pyramid logic as an internal coherence check: establish the situation, surface the complication, state the management question or implication, and lead to the approved answer. Do not use `Situation`, `Complication`, `Question`, and `Answer` as mechanical client-facing headings.

## 5. Section Construction

### 5.1 Executive message

When producing a complete M1, begin with one answer-first sentence that connects the situation to the decision or direction. It should be specific enough that it would not fit an unrelated client.

Recommended logic:

```text
Because [material situation or gap], the organization needs to [management decision or approved direction] so that [intended outcome].
```

Do not write a slogan, generic aspiration, or unsupported conclusion.

### 5.2 Organization introduction and relevant history

Include only facts that explain the decision. Useful content may include:

- origin or major transition;
- current activities, offerings, customers, or markets;
- ownership and group relationships;
- scale or footprint, when verified and relevant;
- strategic ambition or mandate;
- distinctive assets, infrastructure, reputation, or capabilities;
- the event or trend that triggered the proposal.

Connect history to the present rather than listing chronology. A strong introduction answers:

```text
Who is the organization, what has changed or is being pursued, and why is this decision now relevant?
```

Exclude generic promotional language, unrelated milestones, exhaustive product lists, and unverified superlatives.

### 5.3 Current situation and position

Present a balanced position. The section must include material positive conditions as well as constraints whenever both are supported.

Build the section in this order:

1. **Starting position:** relevant performance, structure, market position, operating condition, or mandate.
2. **Enablers:** strengths, assets, capabilities, relationships, or momentum that make progress possible.
3. **Constraints:** gaps, fragmentation, underuse, ambiguity, risk, or misalignment.
4. **Management gap:** the difference between current and desired state.

Choose only problem-relevant lenses. Possible lenses include strategy, market, governance, inter-entity relationships, operating model, process, organization, people, culture, finance, technology, data, customer, or regulatory conditions. These are not mandatory headings.

When relevant:

- distinguish group-level governance from subsidiary-level operating issues;
- distinguish available data from usable management information;
- identify dependence on tacit knowledge or key individuals as a continuity risk;
- preserve stakeholder disagreement instead of averaging it into false consensus;
- state suspected causes as hypotheses, not facts.

Avoid converting the section into a generic SWOT analysis unless the user explicitly requests one.

### 5.4 Consequences and management implications

Consequences must add meaning; they must not restate constraints with stronger adjectives.

For each material consequence, use this logic:

```text
Condition -> mechanism -> affected objective or stakeholder
```

Separate:

- **Observed consequences:** already evidenced in performance, behavior, delivery, cost, decision speed, risk exposure, customer outcomes, or another relevant area.
- **Future risks:** plausible effects if the condition persists; calibrate certainty and time horizon.
- **Management implications:** what management can no longer defer, clarify, or leave unresolved.

Prioritize consequences by decision impact, not drama. Avoid catastrophic language unless the evidence supports it. Do not claim quantified loss, impact, or probability without a verified basis.

### 5.5 Decision question

For M1A and decision briefs, state one governing decision question. Add no more than three supporting questions when genuinely necessary.

A useful question is:

- answerable through the proposed work;
- neutral about the result;
- specific about the choice, scope, or uncertainty;
- material to management.

Examples of form, not reusable content:

```text
Should the organization proceed with [choice], given [critical criteria]?
```

```text
How should [operating or governance challenge] be redesigned to enable [desired outcome] while respecting [constraint]?
```

Do not disguise a predetermined answer as a question.

### 5.6 Proposed solution and phase summary

This subsection has one canonical source: approved M2.

When M2 is approved:

- summarize the solution direction in one short paragraph;
- preserve approved phase names, sequence, boundaries, and intended outcomes;
- explain how the phase logic addresses the management gap rather than listing phase names without an implication;
- mention a critical condition or sponsor dependency only when material;
- omit detailed activities, deliverables, pricing, and timelines unless the user explicitly requests them in M1.

When M2 is absent or not approved:

- omit the subsection; or
- label it `Provisional solution direction — pending consultant approval`;
- describe only a directional hypothesis needed for discussion;
- do not invent final phase names or commitments.

M1B must insert the approved M2 summary without rewriting M1A. Reopen M1A only if M2 exposes a material contradiction or previously missing fact.

## 6. Output Schemas

### 6.1 Full executive summary

Use this default order:

```markdown
Artifact: M1 — Executive Summary — vX
State: Draft | Provisional | Approved

Executive message
[One answer-first sentence]

Organization context
[Relevant introduction and history]

Current situation
[Balanced enablers, constraints, and management gap]

Consequences and implications
[Observed consequences, future risks, and management significance]

Proposed direction
[Approved M2 summary, or explicit provisional label]

Decision requested
[What the consultant should approve or amend]
```

Include evidence notes, assumptions, or validation questions after the main narrative only when they are material to approval. Do not interrupt every sentence with internal labels unless the user requests an audit-style output.

### 6.2 M1A diagnostic basis

```markdown
Artifact: M1A — Situation and Implications — vX
State: Draft | Provisional

Executive message
Organization context
Current situation
Consequences and implications
Governing decision question
Material assumptions or validation questions
Approval requested: approve or amend M1A as the basis for Module 2.
```

### 6.3 Decision brief

```markdown
Artifact: M1 — Decision Brief — vX
State: Draft | Provisional

Situation or opportunity
Management complexity or risk
Decision question(s)
Gated approach
Decision requested
```

### 6.4 Partial-section output

Return only the requested headings and content. Add a short `Dependency note` only when an omitted section prevents the requested text from being treated as final.

## 7. Density and Style

Default to formal, fluent, neutral Persian unless the user requests another language. Match terminology already approved in the proposal.

Recommended density:

- `Compact / slide-ready content`: approximately 180–350 words for a complete M1.
- `Detailed review draft`: approximately 400–700 words plus evidence notes when needed.
- `Partial section`: only the length needed to resolve its purpose.

These are guidance, not quotas. Prefer an additional review paragraph over compressing away a material uncertainty; prefer deletion over repeating a point in multiple sections.

Use:

- answer-first phrasing;
- parallel grammar for comparable points;
- specific nouns and active verbs;
- neutral language for reported concerns;
- calibrated terms such as `may`, `is likely to`, or `requires validation` when certainty is limited.

Avoid:

- generic consulting language that could fit any organization;
- promotional biography;
- long source paraphrases;
- unsupported causal certainty;
- mixing consultant credentials into the diagnosis;
- repeating the same fact as context, problem, and consequence;
- detailed method or activity lists in the executive summary.

## 8. Review and Revision Protocol

### 8.1 Logic review

Check that:

- the introduction explains the decision context;
- the current situation contains both relevant enablers and constraints;
- the management gap is explicit;
- consequences follow from stated conditions;
- the decision question follows from the diagnosis;
- the proposed direction comes only from approved M2 or is visibly provisional.

### 8.2 Evidence review

Check that:

- every name, number, date, relationship, and current-state claim has a known basis;
- stakeholder statements are not silently converted into verified facts;
- causal claims show appropriate uncertainty;
- material contradictions remain visible;
- external sources, when used, are listed once rather than repeated by section.

### 8.3 Redundancy review

Assign each statement one primary home:

- identity or decision context -> organization introduction;
- current fact, asset, or constraint -> current situation;
- effect or risk -> consequences;
- intervention -> proposed direction.

Delete duplicate statements. If a later section needs the idea, state only the new implication.

### 8.4 Partial-edit review

When revising one subsection:

- preserve unrequested wording and structure;
- check terminology against protected sections;
- flag contradictions that materially affect the requested text;
- do not use contradiction detection as permission to rewrite other sections.

## 9. Approval and Handoff

For M1A, request explicit approval of:

- organization and scope boundary;
- balanced current-position statement;
- central gap or challenge;
- consequences and management implications;
- governing decision question;
- material evidence limitations.

The approved M1A handoff to M2 contains only:

```text
organization/scope
decision question
desired outcome
enablers
constraints and gap
consequences
critical assumptions and unresolved evidence
```

Do not pass duplicated narrative when this compact handoff is sufficient.

After M2 approval, complete M1B and request approval of the consolidated M1. Do not begin M3, storyline design, or PowerPoint production as part of Module 1.

## 10. Acceptance Tests

Module 1 passes only if it can:

1. create a full executive summary from messy notes without inventing facts;
2. produce M1A without prematurely designing a solution;
3. insert an approved M2 summary without changing approved M1A;
4. return only `Current situation` and `Consequences` when those are the only requested sections;
5. create a decision brief for a gated market-entry or investment question;
6. represent strengths and constraints in the same current-position narrative;
7. separate group-level and entity-level conditions in a multi-entity proposal;
8. distinguish observed consequences from future risks;
9. protect the rest of an existing user-authored proposal during a partial revision;
10. stop at the M1 approval gate with a clear decision request.
